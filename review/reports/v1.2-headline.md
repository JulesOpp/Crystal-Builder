# Turn measured patterns into ranked structures

> **Written 2026-11-06** against `Crystal-Builder` at `94bbb3e` (v1.1.0), from desk research only. It builds on an earlier survey of 2026-10-09 that is not in this directory. Versions and paper claims were true on that date; the notes behind it are in [research-parts/v1.2-headline/](research-parts/v1.2-headline/).

Crystal Builder 1.2 should lead with **Solve**: a panel that takes a measured powder pattern and returns a ranked table of candidate structures, each refined against the data, energy-checked and diagnosed. Its flagship is a **reticular solve**. The user names the node and linker, and the app enumerates nets, builds and relaxes each candidate, then ranks them by Rietveld fit. No published workflow does this. The case is unusually strong because 1.1 already ships most of the pipeline: indexing, Pawley, the RietX Rietveld engine with energies, three ML potentials, PORMAKE with the RCSR nets, and the Rwp-versus-energy view. Every method with credible measured-data results in 2026 follows the same generate, refine, energy-filter recipe, so the missing piece is a candidate generator, a background job and a results table rather than new science. The other three candidates rank behind it, and each feeds it. **Reviewable agent campaigns** come second and are best shipped as the engine Solve runs on, because agent control alone became table stakes in 2026: Avogadro 2.1, OVITO, Matlantis and Schrödinger all shipped or announced it. The **adsorption panel** is the clearest open-source gap, but it needs four new subsystems and the heaviest set of chemistry decisions, so it makes the stronger 1.3 headline. **Generative design** belongs underneath as a candidate source, since no model clears 1% stable-unique-novel output and the leading model is limited to cells of 20 atoms or fewer. The main risk is that no experimental MOF benchmark exists, so Solve must present ranked hypotheses, never answers, and needs a test set Julius chooses before launch. On watermarks, the evidence is one-sided. A citable DOI, an in-app "Cite" prompt and overt metadata will find more papers than any hidden mark. Covert marks in data meet the definition of falsification, and invisible image watermarks do not survive figure assembly. Hidden marks in MIT-licensed code are visible to anyone who reads the source, and a per-install ID turns every shared file into personal data under GDPR. Whether and how to disclose the marks is Julius's call.

## Solve reuses four stages 1.1 already ships, and its fifth is unclaimed

Structure solution from powder data has converged on one recipe: generate candidates, refine each against the pattern by Rietveld, then filter by energy and chemistry. **RealPXRD-Solver** (March 2026) reports **77.9% and 78.8% top-1 on measured CNRS and RRUFF patterns when the lattice is supplied**. That falls to 47.8% and 57.1% on structures absent from training, it handles only cells of 20 atoms or fewer, and its authors call the GSAS-II refinement step "essential" ([arXiv 2603.00965](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00965v1)). Without a supplied lattice, the best ML top-1 on measured data stays at **42% for Crystalyze** ([MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2024/ai-model-can-reveal-crystalline-materials-structures-0919)) and **44.2% for Bragglite** ([curated list](https://github.com/xyin-anl/awesome_pxrd2xtal_generation)). The physics-based **Ab-PXRD-Solver** from the PyXtal group (May 2026) indexes the cell, enumerates Wyckoff combinations, relaxes candidates with MACE at fixed cell, then ranks them by GSAS-II Rietveld. It reports 94–100% success on cubic through orthorhombic cases but **60.1% on monoclinic** ones, on what are likely simulated patterns. In one example it generated about 33,000 trial structures and the true answer ranked ninth; the authors stress it is "not a black-box optimization procedure" ([arXiv 2605.24594](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24594v1)). For known phases, the Ceder group's **Dara** tree-searches database phases and refines each with BGMN. It is MIT-licensed ([GitHub](https://github.com/CederGroupHub/dara)), identified **18 of 20 commercial mixtures from 2-minute scans and 20 of 20 from 8-minute scans** ([curated list](https://github.com/xyin-anl/awesome_pxrd2xtal_generation)), and processed more than 2,400 A-Lab patterns at a median of 89 s each ([AI Agent 2026](https://www.oaepublish.com/articles/aiagent.2026.25)). The lesson across all of them is that generating candidates is now cheap. Judging them is the bottleneck, and a good indexed cell is the strongest single lever.

That is the part Crystal Builder already owns. Its refinement workbench indexes, Pawley-fits, refines with RietX, runs "Rietveld with energies" and plots Rwp against energy. MACE, ORB-v3 and MatterSim run in-process, and PORMAKE ships with its 867 building blocks and the RCSR nets (per the app's `docs/RELEASE_NOTES.md`). Ab-PXRD-Solver reads almost like a blueprint of this stack with RietX in place of GSAS-II. A 1.2 Solve therefore needs three new things: a candidate generator, orchestration as a background job, and a results table. It does not need a new refinement engine.

The fifth stage, a framework-aware candidate generator, is where the white space lies. **No published workflow was found that builds MOF candidates from topologies, ranks them by Rietveld or Pawley fit against measured PXRD, and filters them with a universal ML potential.** The closest, Xrd2Mof (JACS 2025), diffuses over coarse-grained nodes and linker centroids, matches linkers against about 400,000 building blocks, and relaxes with a classical force field. It reaches **93.4% (single-linker) and 96.2% (dual-linker) top-10 on about 10,000 simulated CSD patterns**, with a single measured pattern its authors call "encouraging but not a systematic experimental benchmark". The 2026 highlight of that paper proposes combining it with Rietveld and MLIP ranking only as an idea ([AI Agent 2026](https://www.oaepublish.com/articles/aiagent.2026.25)). XtalNet's MOF results (top-10 of 90.2% on hMOF-100 and 79% on hMOF-400) are also simulated only ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11848617/)). The 20-atom ceiling on the ML solvers rules frameworks out entirely. A reticular chemist holding a new powder pattern currently has no open end-to-end tool. The reticular solve is tractable for a reason the generic solvers cannot use: the chemist usually knows which node and linker went into the flask. That shrinks the search from all of chemistry to nets times interpenetration, which PORMAKE already enumerates. This is an inference about the workflow, not a measured result.

The competitive timing favours a solve headline over an agent headline. **Avogadro 2.1.0 (23 October 2026)** added a JSON-RPC command interface, a new `avogadro-mcp` project for connecting LLM agents, CIF space-group perception and batch ML-potential energies ([release](https://github.com/OpenChemistry/avogadroapp/releases/tag/2.1.0)). OVITO markets its Claude Code, Copilot CLI and Codex integration as a Pro feature ([ovito.org](https://ovito.org/)). Matlantis brought Claude Code into its platform in May 2026 ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/27/3301998/0/en/Matlantis-Brings-Claude-Code-Into-Atomistic-Simulation-Platform-Releases-Public-Skills-Library-on-GitHub.html)). "Your AI assistant can drive the app" no longer distinguishes Crystal Builder. Structure solution, by contrast, is sold only in commercial packages: CrystalDiffract's integrated Phase ID ([CrystalMaker](https://crystalmaker.com/crystaldiffract/)) and Mercury's licence-gated, molecular-oriented PXRD Optimize ([CCDC](https://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/2025-csd-releases/)). CrystalMaker keeps moving toward porous users with MOF Automation in 11.7.0 ([release notes](https://crystalmaker.com/crystalmaker/release-notes/mac/11/index.html)). Its release history mentions no topology-based solve, but an open tool that gets there first would set the reference. A reticular solve is also a publishable method paper, and the watermark section below shows that a citable paper is the single most effective way to see who uses the app.

The strongest case against Solve is that it may not work on real framework data, and nobody yet knows. Measured MOF patterns carry the effects Xrd2Mof's own authors list as missing from simulation: broadening, preferred orientation, background, guests, defects and breathing ([AI Agent 2026](https://www.oaepublish.com/articles/aiagent.2026.25)). A 2026 hierarchical ZIF structure-prediction study found "considerable variations" in where the correct match ranked by PXRD similarity, and blamed one poorly ranked structure on its lack of porosity ([arXiv 2601.05097](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05097)). So the ML energy should act as a filter that rejects unphysical candidates, not as the judge. The A-Lab critique concluded that automated Rietveld analysis is "not yet reliable" ([Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/challenges-in-high-throughput-inorganic-materials-prediction-and-/)), and press coverage summarised it as "no new materials have been discovered" ([Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article)). LLMs score worst on exactly these steps. In XRDBench's 1,340 runs across 10 models, they averaged **57.8/100**, and were weakest at indexing and Rietveld action selection ([arXiv 2609.00070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00070)). The design consequences are firm. The solve itself must be deterministic code, not an agent improvising refinements. Every result must show runner-ups with their Rwp and energies. The copy must say "consistent with the pattern", never "solved".

## Solve outranks campaigns, adsorption and generation on six tests

The table scores the four candidates the research surfaced on the criteria that matter for a small open-source app with one chemist making the science calls. "Already shipped" counts components in 1.1. "Julius's load" counts the chemistry defaults that must be his.

| Candidate | Open-source white space | Already shipped in 1.1 | Measured-data evidence | New dependencies | Julius's chemistry load | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solve** (pattern to ranked structures; reticular flagship) | Reticular solve unpublished anywhere; inorganic solve only in commercial GUIs | Indexing, Pawley, RietX, energies, MLIPs, PORMAKE, Pareto view | Strong for known phases (Dara); moderate for small inorganic cells; none for MOFs | Little: orchestration and a table | Moderate: energy window, guest handling, benchmark set | **1** |
| **Reviewable agent campaigns** | No desktop GUI found with an agent-planned batch and a per-candidate review table | Typed verbs, diagnostic codes, undo, MCP; `plan` in the backlog | Typed pipelines beat a coding agent on cost and variance (one task, n=5) | MCP Tasks, a job runner | Low: ranking defaults | **2**, as Solve's engine |
| **Adsorption panel** (structure to Henry coefficient, isotherm, BET) | No open desktop GUI runs structure to isotherm | Zeo++, EQeq charges, UFF typing | Methods mature; laptop runtimes unmeasured | RASPA3, ML charges, fugacity, equilibration, BET fit | Heavy: force field, guest models, charges, cutoff | **3**, the 1.3 headline |
| **Generative design** | Crowded; MatterGen and CrystaLLM are open | The validation and relaxation loop | S.U.N. under 1%; flagship validation disputed | PyTorch stack, 0.5 GB weights, GPU-oriented | Heavy: novelty and stability claims | **4**, an engine underneath |

**Campaigns are the right architecture but the wrong headline.** The best evidence for them is El Agente Potente (September 2026). It runs typed execution graphs in which LLMs only plan and route, while deterministic code computes and validates. All 3,000 runs in a 1,000-crystal, three-model MLIP benchmark completed successfully. Failed candidates are returned as typed records instead of halting the campaign ([arXiv 2609.14840](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14840)). On one HKUST-1 CO₂ GCMC task, run five times each, it beat Claude Code with skills on both accuracy and cost:

| Run by | CO₂ uptake (mmol/g) | Heat of adsorption (kJ/mol) | Token cost per run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Code with skills | 1.25 ± 0.57 | 13.81 ± 0.77 | $0.638 ± 0.139 |
| El Agente Potente | 1.88 ± 0.12 | 22.79 ± 0.53 | $0.152 ± 0.003 |

**Only two of the five Claude Code runs considered pre-relaxing the framework** ([arXiv 2609.14840](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14840)). The benchmarks explain why the protocol, not the model, should own geometry. In AtomWorld, LLM rotation edits succeed **under 12%** of the time and supercell success ranges from **4% to 98%** by model, with "largely random" partial edits ([arXiv 2510.04704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04704)). In 619 annotated chemistry tasks, agents ignored evidence at least once in **68%** ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/ai-ignore-evidence-trust-science)). Potente's "successful" campaigns still contained silent physical errors, including H₂ that dissociated during an adsorption relaxation and an MLIP reaction energy with the wrong sign against DFT ([arXiv 2609.14840](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14840)). Potente has no GUI and no approval gates. A 2026 survey of AI-scientist systems argues that an oversight step which records only accept or reject gives little evidence that anyone actually checked ([arXiv 2608.05179](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05179v1)). A review table with diagnostic codes, energies and a thumbnail on every row is the stronger design, and no desktop app was found offering one. So why not headline it? A campaign needs a protocol worth running, and "campaigns" is a hard thing to demo to an experimentalist. It also lands in the most crowded message of 2026. Solve *is* a campaign: N candidates, a fixed typed protocol, checks on every row and a ranked table. Shipping the job engine and results table as the machinery under Solve, with a typed `solve` verb over MCP, delivers the campaign idea with a concrete first use. General agent-planned campaigns, the `plan` item already in the app's backlog, can follow in a point release using the same table.

**Adsorption is the clearest gap but the largest build.** The only end-to-end GUI path found is commercial. SCM's own tutorial has the user hand-compute chemical potentials from JANAF tables, freeze the host manually, and wait: "the calculation of the full isotherm on IRMOF-1 can take several days to converge" ([SCM tutorial](https://www.scm.com/doc/Tutorials/MolecularDynamicsAndMonteCarlo/GCMCMOFIsotherm.html)). RASPA3 ships PyPI wheels for Apple silicon, Intel macOS and Linux as of v3.0.30 ([releases](https://github.com/iRASPA/raspa3/releases)). Its bindings need Python 3.12 or later, which matches the app's 3.12 CI and bundle builds but not its declared `>=3.11` minimum. The bridge to the lab is a simulated N₂ BET area. A round robin of about 60 labs analysing the same isotherms produced widely varying BET areas ([BETSI, ChemRxiv](https://www.doi.org/10.26434/CHEMRXIV.14291644.V1)). The field recommends comparing experimental BET with *simulated* BET rather than geometric surface area ([Gómez-Gualdrón et al., JACS 2016](https://peeref.com/works/949185)). BETSI, the MIT-licensed reference implementation, takes only a CSV ([GitHub](https://github.com/fairen-group/betsi-gui)), so nothing connects a simulator to it. Against that, an adsorption panel needs RASPA3 integration, a charge model, automatic fugacity, equilibration detection and a BET fit, all new. Each embeds a chemistry choice. Charges silently decide polar-guest results: the ML charge model MEPO-ML cut CO₂ uptake error to **0.059 mmol/g against 0.206** for an empirical scheme, at about 2 s per MOF on one CPU core, but states no software licence ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11412901/)). The MIT alternative, PACMOF2, pins Python 3.9 ([GitHub](https://github.com/snurr-group/pacmof2)). A 2026 Chem. Sci. study found that relaxing the full cell with a universal MLIP shifted ethylene affinity by up to **20 kJ/mol** ([RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/Content/ArticleLanding/2026/SC/D6SC00831C)). Universal MLIPs also show systematic over- or under-estimation of adsorption energetics ([arXiv 2602.13725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13725)). No laptop runtime for a RASPA3 Widom calculation or a 77 K N₂ isotherm was found, and the latter converges slowly near pore filling. That is a 1.3 headline with a measured scope. For 1.2, the backlog's RASPA input writer and a reserved "Henry coefficient" column in the Solve table would set it up.

**Generative design fails as a headline on both evidence and reputation.** MatterGen is the strongest open option: MIT code and weights, and the only released property-conditioned checkpoints alongside CrystaLLM-π. But it is trained only on cells of **20 atoms or fewer**, its Apple-silicon support is "experimental" ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/mattergen)), and it takes about **9 s per structure on an A100** ([arXiv 2501.02144](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.02144)). Across 12 models in LeMat-GenBench, stable-unique-novel rates "never exceed 1%" ([arXiv 2512.04562](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04562v1)), and in PhononBench even the best models are only about **45–58% dynamically stable** ([arXiv 2512.21227](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21227v1)). MatterGen's flagship synthesis, TaCr₂O₆, has been challenged in *Materials Horizons* (2026) as a disordered version of a compound reported in 1972 and present in its training data ([RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2026/mh/d6mh00268d)). The best MOF generator, Orbital's Mofasa, reaches 59.9% MOFChecker validity, but no public weights were found ([arXiv 2512.01756](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01756v1)). Simple baselines such as ion exchange from known compounds are competitive at finding stable materials ([arXiv 2501.02144](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.02144)). A "generate new materials" button would invite the very claim the field is debunking. As a candidate source feeding Solve's table, with every output labelled a hypothesis, the same models are useful.

## A credible 1.2 ships two solve routes into one reviewable table

The first version should be two routes, one table and one job runner. Both routes start from a pattern the user has already indexed in the refinement workbench.

**Route A, "rank these candidates"**, is the dependable workhorse. Candidates come from open tabs, a folder of CIFs, or COD and CoRE MOF by formula once the backlog's *Fetch a structure* item lands. Each is matched to the indexed cell, checked by Pawley, refined with RietX, given an MLIP energy and run through the existing diagnostics. Dara-style multi-phase tree search is a natural extension. Dara's MIT code can be read and its search rebuilt over RietX, rather than adding its compiled BGMN refinement engine to every platform build when RietX already does the refining.

**Route B, "reticular solve"**, is the headline. The user picks one or more nodes and linkers from the PORMAKE library or their own blocks, and the app enumerates compatible RCSR nets and interpenetration degrees. Before building anything, it discards cells inconsistent with the indexed lattice, which is the cheap filter. It builds the survivors, pre-relaxes them with UFF4MOF and then relaxes them with the chosen MLIP; the app's TODO records that pre-relaxation cutting MIL-53 scan points from 467 s to 196 s. Finally it applies the MOF diagnostics and ranks by RietX Rwp, with energy acting as a window that rejects outliers rather than as the ranking key.

Both routes write rows to one **results table**. Each row carries a thumbnail, the net or source, the space group, the cell mismatch, Rwp, a relative energy labelled with its engine and dispersion setting, the diagnostic codes and a status. Failed candidates stay in the table as rows, following Potente's design. Clicking a row opens the structure as an ordinary undoable document with its refinement attached, and the Rwp-versus-energy plot that already exists becomes the table's chart. The job runs in the background with progress and cancel. Over MCP it is a typed `solve` verb with Tasks, so an agent can start a solve and read the table but cannot improvise the protocol. That also fixes the backlog's known limit, where one failed health probe drops a long call. The run exports a record of versions, engines, settings and every row, which doubles as provenance for a paper's SI.

Several things should stay out of 1.2. The **symmetry-enumeration solve for small inorganic cells** (the Ab-PXRD-Solver route) should wait for a point release: no licence was found on its repository, its CPU runtime is minutes to hours, and its monoclinic success is 60%. **ML structure generators** belong behind a bring-your-own-weights plugin, because RealPXRD-Solver, XRDSol, Crystalyze and deCIFer state no weight licence ([curated list](https://github.com/xyin-anl/awesome_pxrd2xtal_generation)). **GALLOP** solves molecular crystals about 10× more often than DASH on hard cases and reads Pawley output directly ([CrystEngComm](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2021/ce/d1ce00978h)), but it is alpha-quality and GPU-oriented, so it suits an export rather than a bundled component. **Charge flipping** cannot ship at all, because Superflip's licence forbids redistribution ([licence](https://superflip.fzu.cz/download/license_superflip.txt)). **3D electron diffraction** depends on XDS under Linux or WSL ([IUCrJ 2026](https://journals.iucr.org/m/issues/2026/01/00/jf5003/)) and on Windows-only PETS ([cRED guide](https://iverks.github.io/crysm/cred_guide.pdf)), so it should chain in at the CIF level only.

Three gates should pass before the feature is announced. First, measure RietX throughput on tens to hundreds of candidates, which nobody has done. Second, assemble an experimental MOF benchmark from CSD-matched measured patterns, because none is published. Choosing those patterns is Julius's call. Third, run an ablation showing whether the MLIP filter improves rank or just reorders it.

The candidates combine through the table rather than competing for the window:

| Piece | In 1.2 | Next |
|---|---|---|
| Solve | Headline: routes A and B | Symmetry enumeration, multi-phase search |
| Campaigns | The job runner, results table and MCP `solve` verb | Agent-planned campaigns over any verb sequence (`plan`) |
| Adsorption | RASPA input writer; reserved table column | Widom Henry coefficient and Qst column, then isotherm and simulated-BET overlay as the 1.3 headline |
| Generative | Nothing bundled | Candidate source: CrystaLLM-π XRD-conditioned and MatterGen as BYO plugins for inorganic; generated linkers plus PORMAKE assembly for MOFs |

A simulated-BET column would also make the table a second validator alongside Rwp. A large mismatch between simulated and measured BET flags collapse, wrong interpenetration or residual solvent, which complements the pattern fit. For MOFs, the honest generative route is "generate the linker, assemble on a chosen net". That mirrors GHP-MOFassemble and runs on CPU because the costly step is the MLIP relaxation the app already does. Mofasa's 201,926-structure MofasaDB is CC-BY 4.0 ([arXiv 2512.01756](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01756v1)) and could be offered as a fetchable library of generated frameworks rather than an on-device generator.

## Left-field extensions: the same nets, printed, heard and played

The most interesting oblique directions reuse machinery the app already has (nets, Wyckoff symmetry, phonons, PXRD, meshes) for audiences no crystal program serves. The strongest is **nets as engineering lattices**. Engineers now use crystallographic space groups as a design space for mechanical metamaterials. One 2026 preprint enumerates about 1.95 million voxelised unit cells across all 36 cubic space groups ([arXiv 2603.10934](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10934v1)). A 2025 *Science* paper from the Daraio group turned crystalline network geometries into 3D-printed polycatenated "chainmail" that behaves like a fluid at small loads and a lattice at large strains ([Caltech](https://mce.caltech.edu/news/reimagining-chain-mail)). Crystal Builder knows RCSR, which mechanical engineers do not, so "export this net as a printable strut lattice" or "catenate this net" would reach additive-manufacturing groups no chemistry tool targets. **Physical output** is the cheaper cousin. Mercury and Jmol stop at a generic STL ([SERC](https://serc.carleton.edu/teachearth/activities/312015.html); [J. Cheminform.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5122160/)), and magnetic lattice kits are sold only for fixed structures such as NaCl ([W.F. Education](https://wf-education.com/mo110120.html)). A generator that outputs one printable part per symmetry-unique site, with magnet pockets along bond vectors, would be new. A point-cloud export for subsurface glass engraving, a technique already used for molecules ([xTool](https://www.xtool.com/blogs/xtool-academy/engraving-crystal-as-memory-gift)), costs a few hundred lines of code and makes a memorable thesis-defence gift.

The second cluster is **sound and accessibility**. SingingMaterials, a Python package built on STRAUSS, sonifies phonon densities of states and was accepted to ICAD 2026 ([arXiv 2603.29037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29037)). Crystal Builder now computes phonons and patterns, so a "play" button is small. Accessibility is the more substantive opening. Bryan Shaw's group showed students identified 3D protein models by mouth with **85.59%** accuracy ([Baylor](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2021/baylor-study-uses-candy-models-make-stem-imagery-accessible-students-visual)). No crystal software found offers screen-reader-first operation. A deterministic text narration of formula, space group, coordination, net and pores, exposed through Qt accessibility and the MCP server, plus tactile print exports, would be distinctive. The third cluster is **aperiodic order**. The 2023 "hat" monotile shows a graphene-like spectral function with Dirac-like features in tight-binding models ([arXiv 2307.11054](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2307.11054)) and has been fabricated on SiN films ([arXiv 2506.07561](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07561v1)). The app's twisted-bilayer and carbon tooling could decorate hat or Penrose patches and simulate their diffraction.

## Hidden watermarks would find fewer papers than a citation does

The direct answer is that **no mark exists that is both subtle and impossible to get around in an MIT-licensed open-source app**. The licence lets anyone read the export code, fork it and delete the mark. The only marks that cannot be stripped without effort are ones woven into the data values themselves, and that crosses a research-integrity line. The evidence below gives what works, what survives, what covert options cost, and a recommendation. The disclosure decision stays with Julius.

### Citation, not marking, is how peers count their users

Formal citation is the channel that measures usage at scale, and it works best when a tool offers one canonical reference. VESTA's licence makes acknowledgement a condition of publishing its drawings and points to a single J. Appl. Cryst. paper ([licence mirror](https://data.gpo.zugaina.org/gentoo-zh/licenses/VESTA)). OVITO asks for a citation and reports being "cited in more than 20,000 research publications" ([ovito.org](https://ovito.org/)). Mining informal mentions is far weaker. An evaluation of two software-mention datasets found **54% and 23% of mentions were not to software at all**, and **65.4% of automatically retrieved links pointed to the wrong software**. Its authors conclude that software "should be cited following the software citation principles" ([arXiv 2402.14602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14602)). Large mapping studies settle on the commonest tools: one 2026 study of 1.3 million publications covers only 520 tools ([arXiv 2604.23820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23820)). Crystal Builder lacks the ingredient VESTA and OVITO share. It has no `CITATION.cff`, no DOI and no paper (local check; the repository is MIT, created 2026-08-29, with 55 stars). "Crystal Builder" is also a generic phrase that full-text search will match noisily. GitHub turns a root `CITATION.cff` into a "Cite this repository" button, and Zenodo can mint a DOI per release ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-citation-files)).

### Overt marks survive deposition but not figure assembly

The app already writes the crystallographic community's accepted provenance channel. `xtal/io/cif_writer.py` stamps `# Generated by`, `_audit_creation_method` and `_audit_creation_date` into every CIF. `_audit_creation_method` is a core-dictionary item for naming the creating program ([IUCr](https://www.iucr.org/__data/iucr/cifdic_html/1/cif_core.dic/Iaudit_creation_method.html)), and IUCr co-editors actively curate the `_computing_*` software references ([Acta Cryst. C](https://journals.iucr.org/c/issues/2011/01/00/me0438/)). The mark survives where a CIF is deposited unchanged, which is the likely path for model structures in SI. It is correctly replaced when an experimental structure is re-refined elsewhere. COD holds hundreds of thousands of entries, harvests CIFs from journal sites and is bulk-downloadable by rsync ([NAR](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/40/D1/D420/2903497)). A periodic grep for "Crystal Builder" there is a real, scalable detector. No published count of program names in deposited CIFs was found, so the first scan would also set the baseline. For images, PNG text chunks carry a standard "Software" key, and Matplotlib writes it by default ([PNG spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/png-3/); [Matplotlib](https://matplotlib.org/stable/api/_as_gen/matplotlib.pyplot.savefig.html)). That precedent shows overt figure metadata is uncontroversial. It is also fragile. It is lost when a panel is composited in Illustrator or PowerPoint, and platforms strip even C2PA manifests: a Washington Post test found "No platform that The Post tested kept the Content Credentials data" ([softwareseni summary](https://www.softwareseni.com/how-c2pa-content-credentials-work-and-what-their-limits-are/)). No study was found measuring whether author-supplied figure metadata reaches the published PDF at any specific publisher.

| Channel | Survives into a paper? | Detectable at scale? | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citable DOI, paper and in-app "Cite" prompt | Yes, by design | Yes: Scholar, OpenAlex, citation counts | None |
| CIF `_audit_creation_method` (already written) | When the CIF is deposited unchanged | Yes: COD grep | None |
| `#` header in .xy/CSV; comment lines in XYZ and POSCAR | Rarely; lost on re-plot | No | None |
| PNG/SVG/PDF "Software" metadata | Raw SI files only; lost on compositing | Barely | None if documented |
| Invisible pixel watermark | Low: downscaling, JPEG re-encoding, labels, vector SVG | Only by bulk PDF harvesting | Integrity, reputation |
| Numeric fingerprint in data values | Destroyed by rounding or re-gridding | No | Falsification |
| Per-install or per-user ID in any mark | As its carrier | As its carrier | GDPR, ePrivacy, reputation |

### Covert marks fail on integrity, robustness, discoverability and law

**Data integrity** rules out the only hard-to-strip option. US regulation defines falsification as "changing or omitting data or results such that the research is not accurately represented in the research record" ([42 CFR 93.103](https://www.gpo.gov/fdsys/pkg/CFR-2020-title42-vol1/pdf/CFR-2020-title42-vol1-sec93-103.pdf)). Journal image policies forbid undisclosed alteration regardless of magnitude ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/authors/policies/image.html)). A fingerprint hidden in the low digits of a pattern would make every user unknowingly publish altered data, and `np.round(y, 2)` would erase it anyway. **Robustness** undermines invisible image watermarks. Benchmarks built for full-resolution AI images find the leading schemes break under regeneration and editing attacks ([WAVES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08573); [W-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18775v2)), and a 2025 ensemble attack raised success against StegaStamp by a further 18.7% ([arXiv 2509.03006](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03006v1)). Scientific figure panels are harsher still: small, flat-backgrounded, labelled over and often vector. Detecting a mark would mean harvesting and image-extracting the literature's PDFs.

**Open-source discoverability** means "covert" can only mean "undocumented". Anyone reading a diff will find the mark, and the precedents show how that discovery gets framed. **Microsoft Word's document GUID** helped trace the Melissa virus to its author ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/748462/security-melissa-virus-turning-15-age-of-the-stripper-still-unknown.html)). **Colour-printer tracking dots** became an EFF cause and resurfaced in the Reality Winner case ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2017/06/printer-tracking-dots-back-news)). **Audacity's 2021 telemetry plans and privacy policy** got it labelled "possible spyware" and spawned forks ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2021/07/05/audacity-possible-spyware); [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/audacity-fiasco-fork/)). The counter-example is Homebrew, whose analytics are documented, store no IP addresses and turn off with one command ([Homebrew docs](https://docs.brew.sh/Analytics)). **The law** draws the last line. A constant mark (program name and version) identifies software, not people. A per-install or per-user identifier in shared files is an "online identifier" that can be personal data under GDPR Recital 30 ([gdpr-info.eu](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-30/)). EU regulators' draft guidance also reads ePrivacy Article 5(3) to cover unique persistent identifiers ([Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2023/global/draft-guidelines-on-the-scope-of-cookie-rules-edpbs-expansive-interpretation)). For a solo developer whose project lives on goodwill, those costs far outweigh the few extra papers a hidden mark might surface.

### Recommendation: make citing easy, stamp provenance openly, then measure

Ranked by expected yield, the package starts with **a citable reference**: a `CITATION.cff`, a Zenodo concept DOI, and then the reticular-solve paper or a JOSS or J. Appl. Cryst. note. Next come **in-app citation prompts**, meaning Help ▸ Cite Crystal Builder with BibTeX and RIS and a dismissible one-line "If you publish this, please cite…" in export dialogs. An acknowledgement *request* in the README and About box keeps the licence MIT-clean, where VESTA makes acknowledgement a condition. Third is **overt metadata in every export format**: PNG and SVG "Software" fields, PDF CreatorTool, headers in data files and the existing CIF audit items, never with a per-user identifier. Last is **measurement**, through a Scholar alert on the DOI, periodic OpenAlex queries and a COD grep. A distinctive default render style, with an optional small corner credit that the user can switch off, is the only image "mark" that survives compositing, precisely because it is visible. Julius decides how much of this is disclosed and how. The choices are whether the metadata is documented in the README (the evidence favours documenting it, since that costs nothing and removes the "hidden" framing), whether it is on by default with a Preferences switch, and whether a visible credit exists at all.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the 1.2 question from "which new capability" to "which judge". Generators, databases and agents now produce structures faster than anyone checks them. Every 2026 method with real-data credibility spends its effort on ranking candidates against measurement, and the app's RietX-plus-energies view is already that judge. Solve aims the judge at the problem experimentalists bring to the bench every week. The reticular route then uses the one piece of knowledge generic solvers cannot: the chemist knows what went into the flask. Campaigns, adsorption and generation then stop competing for the headline. They become, respectively, how the judge scales, a further column it reads, and a further source of candidates.

The watermark question turns out to have the same answer from the other side. The most durable "mark" a scientific tool can have is a paper people are expected to cite, and Solve's reticular method is the obvious candidate for that paper. Building the benchmark Julius chooses, measuring RietX throughput and publishing the results would produce the feature, the evidence that it works and the citation channel together.
